Every-other-week recycling and twice-weekly trash

Trash weekly, recycling every other week, and a second trash day in some neighborhoods: city schedules combine the same few patterns in different ways. This guide uses the route data and service notes behind the {{ g.n_cities }} TrashWeek city pages to show which cities use which pattern.

Why the day or week changes from street to street

Collection routes are drawn by the city or its hauler, not by ZIP code. ZIP codes are drawn for mail delivery, so one ZIP code can include several routes, each with its own pickup day and, where recycling alternates, its own week. That is why TrashWeek asks for your address instead of your city.

When a holiday lands in an A/B week

A holiday changes your pickup day; the city's calendar still decides your week. To work out the date:

1. Find your week. Use the week letter or the dated calendar your city publishes. {% if g.ab_cities %}For {% for x in g.ab_cities %}{{ x.c.city }} ({{ x.c.state }}){% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %} the route data carries the A/B label per zone, so the zone page shows it. {% endif %}TrashWeek's data does not include the date each city's week A starts, so these pages cannot tell you which week it is today — the city calendar can.

2. Apply the city's holiday rule. Open the holiday page for your city: a one-day slide moves your pickup one day later inside the same calendar week; published make-up days name the exact date.

3. Trust the city notice when it differs. If the city posts a specific date for the holiday week, that date wins.
